## How the walk numbers are made

- **Steering**: the walker walks the route polyline itself: it turns toward the next vertex only once it is
  within 0.05 m of the current one, so by construction of that reach every vertex is cut by less than 0.05 m in
  plan and the walk never takes a chord across a corner that the line does not take. The same rule is used on
  every route.
- **Walked length**: summed 3D distance the walker's feet travel while following the route polyline waypoint
  by waypoint (and the same sum in plan, which is the *flat* figure). The router measures an edge in 3D when its
  heights are known and in plan when they are symbolic (`geometryLength(geometry, symbolicZ)` in
  `packages/navigation/src/router.ts`), so the walk compares the figure the route was measured in: the flat one
  when the route has a symbolic height, the 3D one otherwise. The **Length basis** column names that figure per
  route, so a plan length is never read as a walked 3D one.
  Every vertex is tied from inside the vertex reach, so by construction of that reach it can add or cut less than
  0.05 m of path; the error is bounded per vertex, not proportionally to the route. The tolerance is therefore
  `max(0.5 m, 0.05 m x polyline vertices)`: the measured value is in the table and a route outside the band is a
  finding, not tolerance noise.
- **Rides**: a lift ride is read from the route itself (#287): inside an `elevator` step, a consecutive polyline
  pair that moves under 0.5 m horizontally while changing height by more than 1.0 m is the shaft, and the same
  vertical span under `walk` or `stairs` is never a ride. The walk takes a ride through the same
  `Guide.take_ride()` the HQ client's E prompt uses, and the **Rides** column shows the count, each shaft's width
  and drop and the total height. The ridden span's plan length and the approach the walking walker could not step
  (the ground ends at the shaft) are excluded from the walked length; a route that states a ride the walk did not
  take fails, so no leg is ever teleported.
  This station's routes have no `elevator` step, so nothing here rides one.
- **Stopped**: a leg that makes no horizontal progress for 60 steps (1 s) or that exceeds twice its
  straight-line walking time plus 600 steps. No route may stop: a stopped route fails the test.
- **Body-block episodes**: runs of steps the walker's own body test (`bodyBlock`: radius 0.3 m, rays at 0.5 / 1.2 m)
  refused. Slide moves along the surface the walker hit are not blocks. No route may have one; a failure names
  the collider that refused the step, the walker's feet where it stood and the clearance to the surface's plane.
- **Floor-band crossings**: every sample of the walker's own height is put in the band of the station floor
  whose elevation is nearest, and every change of band is counted (`floorChanges` counts graph floor transitions,
  so this is an independent observation, not a copy of the route). The walk must start on the route's first
  floor, reach every floor the route names in the order it names them, and end on the route's goal floor. It may
  cross more bands than `floorChanges`, because a stair edge can span floors the route has no node on; every
  such band is one the stair passes through, so it must lie strictly between the elevations of the two
  consecutive route floors being travelled between and move toward the second. A band outside that range, a
  reversal, or a walk that ends on another band fails. The crossed count is shown beside the route's so that
  difference is visible, not averaged away. The elevation is the station's *rendered floor band*
  (`renderElevationM` in `station.json`), not the physical floor level:
  `elevationM` is null on every floor and `elevationSource` is source-derived, unknown.
  The narrowest separation between two different rendered bands here is 4.6 m.
  The walker's height is damped toward the stair surface; a lag smaller than half of that separation cannot
  add or lose a crossing, and no lag figure is asserted here.
- **Walk time**: steps at the web's walk speed 1.45 m/s (60 Hz fixed step); reported, not asserted.
