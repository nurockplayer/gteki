Status: **proposed with Lane C as sole writer; reviewed in-session by both station leads (Ikebukuro, Shinjuku) on 2026-10-20.** Not accepted until merged by the Owner. Types live in [`packages/schema/src/index.ts`](../../packages/schema/src/index.ts); this page explains intent and rules.

## Files a station ships

```text
stations/<id>/gteki/
  station.json   StationManifestFile  {schema, station, sources, floors, coverage, spawnNodeIds?, spawnHeadings?, assets?}
  graph.json     GraphFile            {schema, stationId, nodes, edges}
  pois.json      PoiFile              {schema, stationId, pois}
  scene.ts       optional — exports buildScene(ctx) (@gteki/scene-api)
  assets/**      optional — GLB/PNG/KTX2 referenced by uri (symlinks allowed if they stay inside stations/<id>)
```

`parseStationFiles(station, graph, pois)` must return `ok: true`. It is **fail-closed**: a structural error (missing field, wrong type, NaN) or any truth-rule error yields no bundle and the explorer refuses the station.

`spawnHeadings` is optional and maps spawn node IDs to compass headings in degrees (0 = north, 90 = east, clockwise); stations use it to face their station-identity board at spawn. Values must be finite and within [-360, 360]. Without it, walk entry faces along the route.

## Frames

- **Station frame:** metres; `x` east, `y` north on the ENU tangent plane at `station.origin`; `z` = source height − `origin.hM` (no curvature term). With `hM: 0` and a TP source, `z` stays a TP orthometric height. `StationFrame.toLocal / toGeodetic` roundtrip < 1 mm (tested).
- **Renderer frame** (glTF, Three.js, Godot — all Y-up, right-handed): `(x, z, -y)`; `ENU_TO_RENDERER_MATRIX` is the column-major form. GLBs are authored in the renderer frame (identity) or carry `assets[].transform`.
- **Unknown heights stay unknown:** `position.z: null`. `Node.renderZ`, `Floor.renderElevationM` and `Edge.geometryZ: "symbolic"` are display-only and never used as surveyed facts.

## Renderer-neutral presentation

`routePolyline(bundle, route)` and `describeRoute(bundle, route, "ja" | "en")` give every client the same polyline and turn-by-turn text. Up/down is said only when evidenced (known z, known elevation, or same-operator ordinals), and a chain with a known direction never absorbs a segment whose direction is unknown. Floors are named via `floorDisplayName(floor, lang)`: `displayNames` in that language, then ja, then the exact source label. A walk merged across a choice point (a pass-through node where another branch of the station's traversable graph leaves — closed, unverified and arrive-only one-way edges, and edges that repeat the route's own passage — same two nodes, leaving along the same line — do not count; the query closures and strict-hours setting recorded on `route.profile` apply, and a branch with known hours is dropped only when it is shut at every minute from departure to arrival (it may open by the time the traveller reaches the junction), while traveller preferences such as step-free, avoided modes or fare never hide a visible branch) carries a `junctions` hint: `straight` when the chosen branch is clearly the straightest (no other branch within 30° of its bend) or the straightest middle of a fork, `keep_left` / `keep_right` when every other branch ahead (within 90° of arrival, inclusive) lies on the other side; a lift door counts as a choice but has no direction. The hint is appended to the walk text ("After ~40 m, keep right at the junction.") and names a junction only by the display name of an `exit` or `ticket_gate` POI reached from that node (any other category stays unnamed, so a route heading into a hall is never said to pass it); otherwise it says "the junction". When the choice cannot be stated without guessing (no heading in or out, a non-lift branch too short to have a heading, two branches on the same line, or a fork where the route is neither straightest nor to one side), no hint is given. Walking legs under 5 m (without a junction hint) are not read out alone: a straight step off a flight, lift, gate or walkway is added to it; other short legs fold into the next instruction, their turns listed in walking order in `leadTurns` before its own turn ("Turn left, then right, ~16 m", "Turn left, then left again, then take the stairs up to B1"); short legs right before arrival end the instruction before them via `trailTurns` ("…, then turn right"; after a junction hint, "After ~90 m, turn right." so it cannot read as the hint's), and a route of short walks only keeps its opening leg and folds the rest into it. Nothing is folded into or across a `wait` (nor is a lift ride merged across one): short legs before it end the instruction before them, and a straight first leg after it is kept like the opening leg, while one that turns (from the heading the traveller waited in) leads into the next line like any short leg. The opening leg, which every later turn is measured from, is folded only into a flight, lift, gate or walkway it leads straight onto without a turn of its own. Each turn is still named once, at the junction where it happens: `distanceM` / `stepIndexes` cover the folded legs (every step belongs to exactly one instruction), but the spoken distance and junction `atM` are those of the instruction's own walk, counted from its last turn ("Go straight ~20 m, then turn back" turns back after 20 m; a walkway says its own length). Contiguous lift links (cab ↔ door, one link per floor passed) are one ride, "Take the elevator to B2": up/down only when the floors where it is boarded and left evidence it and no link of the ride says otherwise. A stair or ramp flight that directly follows another (only short legs between) is a continuation ("Continue on the stairs", "引き続き階段を進む"; "Continue up the stairs" only when both flights are evidenced in that sense); a flight that is known to reverse the last one is never called a continuation. Pass the destination POI as a fourth argument (`describeRoute(bundle, route, lang, poi)`, as the web explorer does): when the route ends on one of its connectors that is a `stair_end`, `escalator_end` or `ramp_end` node (and the POI is not that stairs/escalator itself), the final `arrive` instruction carries `connector` and says the connector starts there ("Stairs to Exit 4 start here" / "4番出口への階段はここから"; "Route ends at the stairs for …" when the route itself arrived by those stairs) instead of "Arrive at destination". It names only the node kind and the POI display name (lang, then ja, then first, skipping blank names; "your destination" / "目的地" if none) — never a direction or floor, which the graph does not evidence beyond the connector. Without a POI (e.g. the CLI, which routes to node ids) arrival text is unchanged. Otherwise a destination that is not an `exit` says its own `notes` entry in the requested language when it has one (never another language's), and a `ticket_gate` with no such note says it arrived on the public side of the gate, inside of which is not modelled ("Arrived at the public side of the gate (inside the gate is not modelled)" / 「改札外側に到着（改札内は未モデル化）」); other categories keep "Arrive at destination".

## Scene hook (`gteki.scene/0.1`)

`update` runs once per frame before the camera is stepped; `camera`, when given, holds the previous frame's pose (fine for distance culling, not for exact view tests). Hosts that omit it keep the two-argument hook compatible.

`buildScene(ctx) → { root, walkable, colliders?, cameraBlockers?, walls?, spawnPoints?, environment?, update?, dispose }`. A module may also export an optional `releaseCaches()`: the explorer calls it once when the explorer leaves this station for a different one (or is disposed), never between reopens of the same station — drop caches kept across opens (parsed geometry, baked textures) there; a throw is logged and ignored. Use `ctx.THREE` (one Three instance), `ctx.loadGlb`, `ctx.loadTexture` (keeps `uv1` for lightmaps; geometry is never merged). Without a scene module the explorer loads `assets[]` and classifies by `gteki_role` (`floor`/`stairs_ramps` → walkable; `wall`/`barriers`/`transport` → colliders; `camera_blocker` → cameraBlockers, hidden). A URI placed several times is loaded once; later placements are clones sharing geometry and materials. Meshes whose own or inherited role is `sign`/`signs`/`signage` (any scene, module or manifest) stay visual-only: they never block the walker or pull the follow camera in; the explorer instead dithers them away inside a tapered capsule from the camera to the avatar's head, so a sign in between or within the near distance never cuts through the view (Godot tags them `gteki_see_through`). A see-through mesh that is also walkable or a collider is never faded, and only built-in materials used by signs alone are cut (a material shared with a wall, or a custom `ShaderMaterial`, stays solid).

**Camera lens.** The follow camera (walk and guide, third person) uses a 50° vertical FOV with a 3.45 m default boom; first person and overview stay at 60°. On a stair, escalator or ramp (smoothed vertical speed above 0.25 m/s, held 0.6 s after it drops below 0.12 m/s), or while walls or ceilings squeeze the boom under its comfortable distance (1.6 m, released above 1.9 m), the third-person lens widens to 60° so the avatar stays in frame, and eases back to 50° when neither condition applies. Switching modes eases the FOV over about 0.25 s, and a teleport or return to overview jumps straight to the target.

**Station identity boards (opt-in).** Tag a mesh or group with `userData.gteki_identity: IdentityBoardTag` (`@gteki/scene-api`): `{ front?: [x,y,z], regions?: [{ kind: "name" | "arrow", min: [x,y,z], max: [x,y,z] }] }`. Coordinates are object-local; `front` defaults to outward +Z; omitted regions use the whole object's bounds, including descendants, as the name. Explicit regions require a name; malformed tags are ignored. Solid and see-through `sign` boards both work. Only the first walker placement per open may frame a front-readable board within 30 m with clear head-to-name and modelled follow-camera-to-name sight (signs never block), a name box wholly inside the free area clamped to the viewport and at most half the viewport height, and its centre in the free area's central 60% horizontally and the NDC vertical band [-0.4, 0.7] intersected with that area; guide starts preserve body heading and skip boards >90° from the route. The identity QA hook samples the name rect — its centre and four corners inset 10 % on the visible face of the name box, never the whole board, and so a board's own plate never counts even when a merged sign batch draws it outside the tagged subtree — and reports occlusion only when the centre ray blocks or a majority (3 of 4) of the corner rays do, geometry beating sign: the same physical board requires the same parent and either overlapping world bounds or face names `<id>` / `<id>:back`; only those faces are excluded from that hook's occlusion test, while boards under different parents remain occluders. Outside overview, the label re-check hides overlapping unselected chips (counted as hidden) and offsets selected chips above/below name and arrow regions.

**Camera blockers.** `cameraBlockers` (or GLB meshes with the `camera_blocker` role, by extras or by a `camera_blocker` / `camera_blocker_…` name) stop only the follow camera, so it never rises through a ceiling or slab. Under one (a hanging sign box, a low cap) the camera first slides the boom lower, down to about the pivot's level and still aimed at the same target, and is pulled toward the avatar only if no lowered boom has room; walls and floors keep their plain clamp. They add to walkable and colliders for the camera and never replace them. They never block walking, never count as ground, and never hide the sky for the street-backdrop probe. A mesh that is both a collider and a blocker is tested once. They are opt-in: a mesh named `ceiling` is not inferred as a blocker. Godot mirrors the role: hidden, colliding on `CAMERA_LAYER` (layer 2) only. **Sign vs. blocker:** a mesh is never both dithered and a camera stop — the blocker wins. A GLB mesh has one role (its own role beats an inherited one), so a `sign` mesh is never classified as a blocker; when a scene module lists a sign mesh in `cameraBlockers` explicitly, it stops the camera and stays solid (never cut). If the scene throws, the explorer shows the L0 graph view; routing is unaffected.

`environment: { background?, fog?, exposure?, lighting?, envIntensity? }`. The explorer tone-maps with AgX; `exposure` is multiplied by one explorer constant (`AGX_EXPOSURE_GAIN`) so values tuned under ACES still read about the same. In "default" lighting the explorer adds its hemisphere + sun and sets `scene.environment` to a neutral studio map (a PMREM of three's `RoomEnvironment`) at `envIntensity` (explorer default when omitted). The high tier lights `MeshStandardMaterial` with that PMREM environment (diffuse and specular). The low (phone) tier instead uses an SH light probe baked from the same environment: diffuse only, no environment reflections, because per-pixel IBL lookups dominated low-tier frame time (CI, Shinjuku platform→exit2: 2.9 → 5.5+ min). `envIntensity` scales both (the probe's diffuse irradiance matches the PMREM's at equal intensity). `sceneInfo().environment` reports the active path (`"envMap"`, `"probe"` or `null`). `lighting: "scene"` turns off the hemisphere + sun (for baked lightmaps / underground stations) and, unless `envIntensity` is set above 0, leaves `scene.environment` unset so a lightmap is never double-lit; the explorer then lights only its own avatar through a per-material environment map. A scene that sets `envIntensity` above 0 in "scene" lighting opts into the environment light on all its materials (the avatar then uses it too). While `scene.environment` is set, three applies `envIntensity` as the `envMapIntensity` of every material without its own `envMap`, so per-material environment tuning needs the material's own `envMap` (as Ikebukuro's lightmapped materials do). On the high tier in default lighting the explorer also manages `castShadow` and `receiveShadow` on the station's opaque, drawn meshes (keeping any `receiveShadow = true` the scene set), with the sun's shadow following the walker. Geometry whose lowest point is above the walker's head (`CAST_HEADROOM_M`, 2.2 m over their feet), such as ceilings and the slabs of upper levels, does not cast, so interiors keep their own lighting. A mesh or group whose `userData` (a GLB extra lands there too) has `gteki_shadow: "none"` never casts, nor does anything under it (other values are ignored with a console warning; Godot ignores the flag); `sceneInfo().shadows.tallCandidates` counts casters taller than 6 m (merged meshes spanning several levels), which a station should split. The explorer scales every station material's `aoMapIntensity` per quality tier once at attach (x1 on low; x`AO_MAP_HIGH_SCALE` on high, where the screen-space GTAO pass also runs), and an `aoMap` on UV1 (`channel = 1`) needs a `uv1` attribute on every geometry using it, else the explorer disables that map (`sceneInfo().bakedAo`, which also reports the estimated `textureBytes` of the distinct aoMap textures). The scale is keyed on the quality tier, not on whether GTAO runs; today GTAO runs if and only if the high-tier composer exists. three multiplies `aoMap` into the indirect diffuse light, and that includes `lightMap`, so a `lighting: "scene"` station that ships its own `aoMap` on top of an AO-baked lightmap darkens twice: ship one or the other. Overview renders no sun shadows (the cutaway removes geometry only from the colour pass, so cut geometry would still cast). `WALKER_BODY` (radius 0.3 m, body band feet+0.2 … feet+1.7 m, eye 1.62 m, step up 0.45 / down 0.9 m) is part of the contract: a `WallSegment` blocks only when its `zMin…zMax` overlaps the body band, so scenes can author railings above stair runs without blocking them. Changing it requires both leads' check. `WALKER_SPEED = { walkMps: 1.45, runMps: 3.4 }` (also exported from `@gteki/scene-api`) is the walker's ground speed in m/s; the explorer reads it, and replay/timing tools must import it rather than copy or regex the numbers. **Street context replaces the pavement plane.** When the outdoor backdrop is up, the core draws a plain pavement plane 0.15 m under the walker's feet. If the station scene contains an object (group or mesh) with `userData.gteki_street_surface === true` that is attached under the scene root and visible, the core hides that plane, so the station's own road surfaces show at their real heights and are never covered or z-fought. Set the flag only on a subtree that actually draws road or pavement surfaces: it is an explicit flag, not a name or a `gteki_role` (roles are inherited by descendant meshes, and a buildings-only context must not hide the plane), and a GLB can carry it as a node extra. The flagged object may arrive later (an async GLB, or added and removed with camera height): the core re-scans the scene for flagged objects about every 0.5 s, on the frame the walker comes out onto level street, and every frame while the plane is still fading in, so one that arrives as the walker reaches street height cancels the fade before it shows. An object already known is re-checked every frame, so removing it from the scene or hiding it (or any ancestor) brings the plane back immediately; one that is detached from the scene root never counts. Sky and haze fade are unaffected. Scene modules may import `three`, `three/addons/*` or `three/examples/jsm/*`; all resolve to the explorer's single copy through three's `exports` map.

## Web explorer

`pnpm dev` (or `GTEKI_STATION_ROOTS=<other worktree>/stations pnpm dev` for read-only cross-worktree integration). QA hooks on `window.__gteki`: `stations, openStation, setOrigin, setDestination, setProfile, route, instructions, mode, startGuide, quality, avatar, walkFromNode, simulate(seconds, keys), walkPath(points, opts), signClearance(radiusM), sceneInfo, phase, labels, overviewFrame, identity, setRouteMarkersVisible`; `labels()` is `{ shown, faded, hidden, decluttered }` (a label dropped by the overview declutter counts only in `decluttered`); `overviewFrame()` is `{ extentShare, fitShare, bbox: {x0,y0,x1,y1} }` in page CSS px for the routable network as projected now, or null outside overview (`extentShare` = larger bbox side / shorter viewport side; `fitShare` the same for the fitted slice box, about 0.65); `sceneInfo().seeThrough` counts the faded sign meshes. `identity()` returns per board `{ id, inFrame, frontFacing, facingDeg, nameRect: {x0,y0,x1,y1} | null, nameHeightPx, coveredByChip, occluded: "none" | "geometry" | "sign" }`: rects are page CSS px, `inFrame` requires every name region inside the viewport, `facingDeg` is 0° head-on (`frontFacing` requires a front-normal cosine >0.2), height is the union of the name regions (not individual glyph height) and `nameRect` / `nameHeightPx` are null when the board is not in frame (including a name corner behind the camera), coverage includes name and arrow and uses the actual drawn chip rects; the label-occlusion rays sample the name rect (centre plus four inset corners on the name's visible face) and exclude the board subtree itself. Lane F samples this hook over the first 5 s of ordinary spawn play on both stations and desktop/phone; visual captures still verify bilingual text size/readability and ≥3 s unobscured (recognition proxy). `setRouteMarkersVisible(visible)` shows or hides the route's start/destination beacons (their columns and rings) without changing the route or guidance, and stays in effect across route recomputes until called with `true`.
`walkPath(points, opts)` synchronously walks renderer-space points `{x, z, y?}` (metres; `y` optional) through normal keyboard walking, returning `{ done, stuck, timedOut, steps }` and calling `opts.onStep(i)` after each frame. A point counts as reached when horizontal distance is at most 0.35 m and, if `y` is given, `|pos.y - y|` is at most 0.6 m. `stuck` means no waypoint advanced and under 0.1 m of 3D displacement over the last second; reaching `maxSeconds` while still moving sets `timedOut` (never `stuck`); the current or last point is checked before the step limit, so `maxSeconds: 0` at the destination returns `done: true`.
`signClearance(radiusM)` is box clearance around the camera, not a frustum test: it counts camera blockers and cuttable sign meshes by horizontal world-AABB distance whose top is at least 0.3 m below the camera or higher (floor-standing signs are ignored); `inside` is true when the camera is within the box grown by the near plane plus 0.05 m; `gapM` is box bottom Y minus camera Y (positive means above the camera; negative means the camera is at or above the bottom edge).

**Static geometry.** At load the explorer builds one triangle tree per walkable, collider and camera-blocker geometry. It uses these trees for ray tests (walking, ground, street probe, follow camera) and for label occlusion; `sceneInfo().rayTrees` reports `{ trees, triangles, bytes, buildMs }`. Ray hits are exactly three.js's own `Mesh.raycast` results; the tree only skips triangles a ray cannot reach. Moving, rotating or scaling a mesh is fine, because trees are in geometry space. Treat vertex data as fixed after `buildScene` returns. A geometry whose position or index buffers are replaced, copied into or re-laid-out later has no current tree. It is then still ray-tested exactly, triangle by triangle, but **no longer hides labels**. Scenes that animate geometry should use skinned or morphed meshes, which are always tested by three.js directly. Stations on main edit no geometry after load.

**Overview framing.** When the overview opens (station load, the HUD Overview button) the camera is fitted to the **routable slice**: the bounds of every edge the default profile may traverse (display heights, plus the 2.4 m label lift), never the whole scene, so a station's street context or backdrop cannot shrink the view. The view direction is fixed (about 42 degrees up, from the south-east); only the target and distance are fitted, from the real perspective projection of the slice's 8 corners at the current aspect. The slice's larger on-screen extent lands at 65 % of the viewport's **shorter** side (so 50-80 % at 1440x900, 393x659 and 412x839, nothing clipped), centred. A resize refits until the user first orbits, pans or zooms; the route preview (`focusPoints`) fits its own box the same way. `OrbitControls` has no min/max distance, and the fit stays inside the camera's far plane for the real stations. Logic: `overviewFraming.ts` (pure, unit-tested).

**Overview label declutter.** In overview only, POI labels whose screen rectangle (projected anchor, centred, plus the label element's measured size, cached) meets a higher-priority label are **hidden** (display none, so DOM rect probes see no overlap), with 3 px clearance. Priority: origin/destination (always kept, even if close to each other), then exits, ticket gates, everything else; within a class a clear label before a faded one, a routable label before a search-only one, then id. It runs only when the camera, label states or sizes change (at most every 80 ms), over the labels the floor and occlusion rules leave visible, and can only hide more: an occluded label stays faded, a label on another floor stays hidden, and labels stay below the UI (C1). A hidden label returns once it clears an extra 4 px, so labels do not flicker while orbiting. Walk and guide are unchanged. Logic: `labelDeclutter.ts` (pure, unit-tested).

## Godot

See [`packages/godot/README.md`](../../packages/godot/README.md): Godot consumes the same station JSON and the TypeScript router's output (`pnpm tsx packages/cli/src/route.ts <stationDir> <from> <goals>` → `gteki.route/0.1` JSON with `result`, node `path`, edge `polyline` and `instructions.{ja,en}`, all with renderer coordinates); it does not reimplement routing truth. `bash packages/godot/run-proof.sh` runs the headless Godot 4 proof (station load, renderer frame, route transport, runtime GLB role classification).

## Versioning

Breaking changes bump `gteki.station/<major.minor>` and require both station leads' compatibility check before merge. Additive optional fields keep `0.1`.
